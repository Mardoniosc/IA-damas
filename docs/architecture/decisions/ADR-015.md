# ADR-015 — Games: Rules-Core replay, single-judge move analysis, leakage-controlled datasets

- Status: **Accepted** (G13, 2026-11-20)

## Context

G12 produces games (PDN/JSONL). G13 must analyse games and build datasets for the learning
system (G14). Two risks dominate: (1) a game record silently "repaired" by the importer, and
(2) dataset leakage, where the same position, or the same game, sits in both training and test
data.

## Decision

1. **PDN replay through the Rules Core.** The parser skips comments, variations and NAGs. Every
   move is resolved with `parse_move` and applied with `GameState.play`. A failure is reported
   with its ply and reason, and the game is excluded, never repaired. `GameType` 20/26 selects
   the ruleset; anything else needs an explicit ruleset.
2. **Move analysis with one judge engine.** For each non-forced ply: the judge's best move,
   then (if different) cross-evaluation of the best and the played move (own units). Labels
   best/good/inaccuracy/mistake/blunder use **provisional thresholds** (0.10 / 0.50 / 1.50; or
   throwing away a proven result). The judge's self-agreement bias is acknowledged in reports.
3. **Datasets**: one record per position before a move:
   - strategic features from the side to move's view;
   - the **outcome from the side to move's view** as the label;
   - optionally, the mover's own eval.

   **The split is by game** (hashed id). Positions whose canonical form is in `train` are
   **removed** from `val`/`test`. A manifest records split parameters, leakage removed,
   outcome balance and **SHA-256 per file**.
4. **Self-play generator** (`python -m damas_ai.games selfplay`): the in-process internal
   search at fixed depth from balanced random openings. It is deterministic given the seed.
5. **Knowledge base**: migration `0002` adds `games` and `game_positions` (ply, move, optional
   label and regret), linked to `positions`.

## Consequences

- Self-play data reflects the internal engine's own (weak) judgement. Outcome labels are honest,
  but games between weak players are noisy. G14 must evaluate with held-out data and with
  matches (G12), not just loss curves.
- Dataset files are generated artifacts (git-ignored). Their hashes make training runs
  traceable.
