# ADR-012 — Decision layer: minimax normalized regret over cross-evaluated candidates

- Status: **Accepted** (G10, 2026-11-20)

## Context

The vision asks for an independent decision-intelligence layer, not a wrapper that relays one
engine. G7 gives per-engine values in each engine's own units, G9 gives tactical candidates,
and G8 gives an internal engine that is weaker but deterministic. Raw scores are not comparable
across engines (R08).

## Decision

1. **Candidates**: the engines' proposals plus the best forcing shot (G9), capped (default 4).
2. **Evidence**: every engine evaluates every candidate (cross-evaluation, own units).
3. **Veto**: drop a candidate that some engine judges a *proven loss* when that engine has a
   better candidate (unless all would be dropped).
4. **Choice**: minimize the worst **normalized regret** `max_e w_e·(best_e − u_e(m))/tol_e`,
   then the mean, then the number of proposers. Tolerances are per engine (default 0.10 own
   units). Weights default to 1 and are configurable.
5. **Confidence**:
   - `high`: ≥ 2 engines and all accept (normalized regret ≤ 1), with no contradiction;
   - `medium`: a majority accepts;
   - `low`: a single source (Brazilian today), a majority rejects, or a contradiction;
   - `none`: no engine.

   A single legal move is `high` by definition.
6. **Strategic profile is descriptive** (`damas_ai.strategy`). It is reported with each
   decision but **not used to choose**. G10 measured that, once material is controlled for,
   its features correlate only weakly with Scan's eval (tempo and mobility even negatively).

## Consequences

- The decision costs (#candidates × #engines) extra searches (≈ 3–5 s per position at 0.2 s
  per move with 2–3 engines).
- Evidence on 39 hard International positions: the aggregate makes fewer errors than any
  single engine under both judges, and makes no class-changing error (G10 report).
- Brazilian decisions are always `low` confidence until a second independent Brazilian engine
  exists (R01).
