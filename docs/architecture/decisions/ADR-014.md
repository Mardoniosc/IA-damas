# ADR-014 — Benchmarks: Rules-Core-arbitrated games, paired openings, Elo with intervals

- Status: **Accepted** (G12, 2026-11-20)

## Context

Earlier gates measured engines position by position. G12 must measure **playing strength** and
provide the outcome data that G7/G10 lacked for calibrating scores. ADR-008 showed that parallel
runs distort effort, and R20 showed that time-limited engines are non-deterministic.

## Decision

1. **The Rules Core is the arbiter.** Each move goes through `GameState.play`. Game end, draws
   (ruleset), forfeits (illegal move, timeout, crash) and a move-cap adjudication (default 300
   plies → draw) are decided by the platform, never by an engine.
2. **Players**: `EnginePlayer` (any adapter, `go think`, full move history for repetition
   detection; one engine process per player, so no tables are shared even in self-play),
   `RandomPlayer`, and `GreedyPlayer` (a 2-ply materialist). The baselines exist to show the
   benchmark measures strength.
3. **Openings**: seeded random walks (default 6 plies) filtered by the internal engine to be
   balanced (|score| ≤ 0.40 men, depth 4). **Every opening is played twice with colours
   swapped.**
4. **Sequential games**, equal limits per side (ADR-008).
5. **Statistics**: W/D/L, score, Elo difference with a 95% interval (normal approximation on
   per-game scores; an unbounded side is reported as `None`), LOS, draw rate. **Calibration**:
   per player, a logistic fit of the final game result on the player's own eval (fractional
   targets for draws).
6. **Outputs**: PDN (with `GameType` 20/26, FEN, termination and opening tags), JSONL with
   per-move info, and `summary.json` with provenance (engine versions, openings, seed, ruleset).

## Consequences

- Small matches give wide Elo intervals. The report states the intervals, not just point values.
- Brazilian results use provisional draw rules (G2), which affects drawn endings.
- Games are not stored in the knowledge base yet. That is the job of G13 (game analysis and
  dataset).
